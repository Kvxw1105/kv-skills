# Humor Mechanisms Reference

## The core model

A useful working formula:

**humor = shared reality + controlled expectation break + safe landing + timing**

- **Shared reality**: The audience recognizes the situation.
- **Expectation break**: The line turns the interpretation, status, scale, logic, or frame.
- **Safe landing**: The violation feels playful, not threatening or cruel.
- **Timing**: The funniest word or image lands last, with no explanation after it.

## Primary engines

| Engine | How it works | Best for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incongruity / mismatch | Set one expectation, reveal another frame | jokes, captions, openers, stand-up style | low-medium |
| Benign violation | Break a social norm while keeping it safe | teasing, roast, edgy social posts | medium-high |
| Status play | Someone's dignity inflates or collapses | sitcom, workplace satire, self-deprecation | medium |
| Superiority-lite | Laugh at foolishness, incompetence, vanity | parody, satire, character comedy | medium-high |
| Relief | Release pressure around awkwardness, stress, taboo | apologies, tense work moments, dating | medium |
| Escalation | Repeat the pattern and make it larger, stranger, or more specific | sketches, 相声三番四抖, sitcom scenes | low-medium |
| Anti-climax | Build grandeur, end with something petty | social posts, titles, punchy replies | low |
| Literalization | Treat metaphor, politeness, or idiom literally | chat replies, absurd comedy | low |
| Over-specificity | Add oddly precise details that imply a whole world | modern Chinese/English internet humor | low |
| Understatement | Describe dramatic situations flatly | dry humor, British-style wit, deadpan | low |
| Callback | Reuse an earlier phrase in a new frame | long posts, speeches, sketches | low |
| Analogy collision | Compare two distant worlds with one shared flaw | essays, speeches, commentary | low-medium |
| Wordplay / homophone | Use sound, ambiguity, segmentation, puns | Chinese memes, slogans, captions | low but can feel cheap |
| Role logic | Let a character be sincerely wrong | sitcom, dialogue, sketch | low-medium |
| Misrecognition | A misunderstands B, B corrects, A misunderstands harder | 相声, two-person banter | low-medium |

## Chinese humor levers

1. **包袱**: organized setup and release. Treat it as a process: 系包袱 -> 垫包袱 -> 抖包袱. Do not dump punchlines without groundwork.
2. **三番四抖**: repeat a misunderstanding or logic three times, then release on the fourth with a sharper turn.
3. **捧逗关系**: one side pushes the absurd logic, the other side represents common sense, correction, disbelief, or reluctant cooperation.
4. **现挂感**: make the line feel like it came from the immediate scene. Refer to a concrete word, object, mistake, timestamp, or prior sentence.
5. **谐音 / 拆字 / 语义滑坡**: useful in Chinese, but only when the wordplay is natural. Forced homophones feel like 春晚作业.
6. **反差身份**: serious role says childish thing; ordinary person uses grand political/academic language for tiny problems; tiny object gets epic treatment.
7. **互联网梗**: use only when the audience shares the meme pool. Otherwise explain the meaning through context, not through a footnote.

## English humor levers

1. **Rule of three**: two items establish the pattern; the third breaks it.
2. **Deadpan**: absurd content delivered with straight, practical wording.
3. **Self-deprecation with status control**: confess a flaw without begging for approval.
4. **Callback**: strong in speeches, threads, and sitcom-style scenes.
5. **Misdirection**: punchline forces reinterpretation of the setup.
6. **Understatement**: describe disaster as a mild inconvenience.
7. **Specificity**: "I was tired" is bland; "I opened the fridge and waited for it to mentor me" has image and character.

## Humor identity spectrum

| Style | Feeling | Use when | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 温和幽默 | friendly, warm | workplace, clients, public posts | user wants sharpness |
| 俏皮 | light teasing | friends, flirting, comments | serious apology |
| 逗比 | silly, self-aware, overcommitted | casual chat, short video copy | formal authority needed |
| 冷幽默 | flat, dry, quiet | intellectual, understated persona | audience needs obvious emotion |
| 毒舌 | sharp, compressed, status attack | satire, close friends, roasts | power imbalance, sensitive target |
| 黑色幽默 | laughs near pain or absurdity | essays, art, private coping | real trauma, public crisis |
| 荒诞 | logic collapses beautifully | memes, surreal posts | direct persuasion |
| 相声感 | rhythmic two-person misunderstanding | scripts, banter, explainers | short serious message |
| 情景喜剧感 | character flaw creates repeated conflict | dialogue, scenes, brand characters | one-line reply |
| 脱口秀感 | personal observation + stance + punch | monologue, article hook | purely informational copy |

## Punchline checklist

- Does the setup create a clear expectation?
- Is the turn surprising but still understandable?
- Is the target safe and socially appropriate?
- Is the funniest word, image, or status drop at the end?
- Can one word be cut?
- Does it sound spoken, not generated?
- Is the joke doing a job: bonding, relieving pressure, teasing, persuading, or entertaining?
