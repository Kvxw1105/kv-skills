# Examples

## Example 1: Student AI planner

Initial thesis: students will pay for an AI planner that creates study schedules.

Actor correction:

- User and beneficiary: student.
- Possible payer: parent or school.
- Blocker: teacher, school IT, or student resistance.

Demand risk: schedule creation is easy and often not the real bottleneck. The stronger job may be adherence, anxiety reduction, or proof of progress.

Experiment: manually run a two-week accountability and replanning service for a narrow exam cohort. Require a deposit, daily check-in data, and repeated use. Compare adherence and parent willingness to continue.

Possible signal: `REFRAME_ROOT` or `SPLIT_USER_PAYER`.

## Example 2: Legal workflow agent

Problem: lawyers repeatedly turn client materials into chronologies and issue lists.

Wedge: one litigation stage, one document type, one jurisdiction, human-reviewed output.

Product form: concierge report or narrow assistant before full automation.

Distribution: legal AI content, professional communities, pilot through small firms, or partnership with existing service providers.

Kill criterion: targeted firms will not share redacted sample data, allocate reviewer time, or pay for a pilot despite recent manual burden.

## Example 3: Useful idea with no channel

A highly accurate niche analytics tool solves a real problem, but the operator has no audience, partner, search demand, or direct access to users.

Do not conclude the product is worthless. Return `REDESIGN_DISTRIBUTION` and test service-led, partner-led, content-led, or workflow-integration entry before building scale features.
